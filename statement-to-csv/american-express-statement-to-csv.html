Worth knowing

Specific to American Express statements

Every institution prints something that trips up a naive conversion. These are the ones that come up with American Express.

Merchant detail runs to several lines

Amex prints the merchant, then the location, and often a reference or category on the lines beneath. Those continuation lines are merged into the description automatically, so a single row keeps the full merchant detail instead of losing it.

Each cardholder gets a section

Supplementary cards are listed under their own headings, so the output is grouped by cardholder. The name itself is a heading with no date and does not become a row.

Foreign spend adds a conversion line

The original amount and currency print below the charge and are merged into the description, so the original figure is preserved in the CSV.

Questions

About American Express conversions

Is my American Express statement uploaded anywhere?

No. The conversion runs inside your browser tab using a PDF engine loaded with the page, and there is no server behind this site to receive a file. You can confirm it: open your browser's network panel before converting and you will see no request carrying the document. Once the page has loaded you can disconnect from the internet and it still works.

Which American Express statements does this work with?

Any American Express credit card statement saved as a PDF with a text layer, which is what you get when you download one from online banking. It is not written against a fixed American Express template — it reads the layout of the page itself, so it keeps working when the statement design changes. Scanned or photographed statements are images with no text to read and need OCR first.

Why not just download CSV from American Express?

Where that covers the period you need, do that instead — it is fewer steps. Amex offers downloads for recent periods; earlier statements are PDF only. Converting the statement PDF is the route to periods the direct download will not reach, and it produces a file that matches the statement a lender or accountant has already seen.

Disclosures

  1. Scanned documents cannot be read. A PDF containing only page images has no text layer to extract. Apply OCR before converting.
  2. Password-protected files must be unlocked first. Remove the password in your PDF reader, then convert the unlocked copy.
  3. Statements written as prose will not parse. The parser requires dated rows carrying amounts; narrative summaries have neither.
  4. Automatic column reading is a starting point. Roles, number format and date order are inferred and presented for your correction. Check them against the statement before exporting.
  5. Verify totals before filing. This is a conversion tool, not a bookkeeping system, and no output should be relied upon for tax or audit purposes without being reconciled against the original document.
  6. This service is not a bank and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of any financial institution whose statements it converts.
